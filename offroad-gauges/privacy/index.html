Privacy Policy

Offroad Gauges — APE Studios

Effective date: 20 August 2026 Developer: APE Studios Contact: apestudios.io@gmail.com Published at: https://ape-studio.github.io/legal/offroad-gauges/privacy/

Offroad Gauges is a gauge cluster for off-road driving: tilt dials, compass, speed, altitude, and optional trip recording. This policy describes what the app does with your data.

The short version: if you do not sign in, your location and your recorded trips stay on your device. If you do sign in, your recorded trips — including their routes — are backed up to our database so they come back on a new phone, and nothing else changes. We also collect anonymous information about the app itself — that it was installed, what kind of phone it is on, and which screens get used — so we can tell whether the app works. None of that is your location.

Signing in is optional. The app offers "continue as guest", and a guest's drives are never uploaded.

How to delete your account and your data

There are two ways, and either one erases everything we hold for you — your account, every trip backed up to it, and the record of this install.

In the app. Profile → the gear → Account → Delete my account. It asks once to be sure, and then it is done immediately. Trips saved on the phone itself stay on the phone; you can delete those from the Trips tab.

By email, if you no longer have the app. Write to apestudios.io@gmail.com from the address you signed in with and ask us to delete your account. You do not need a reason, and you do not need to reinstall anything. We will confirm when it is done.

If you used the app as a guest we may be unable to find your record from an email alone, because a guest record is not linked to any address — that is a consequence of collecting as little as we do, not a refusal. Uninstalling ends a guest record permanently in any case.

What stays on your device

Precise location (GPS). Used while the app is open to display speed, altitude, and heading, and — only when you start a recording — to build the route of a trip. Also used to work out the sun's position for the sunset readout and the automatic day/night theme.

Your trips, if you have not signed in. Recorded trips, including their routes, are written to the app's own private storage. As a guest there is no copy anywhere else, and we cannot see them. Signing in changes this — see Trip backup below.

Your settings never leave the phone, including the approximate last position the sunset calculation needs.

Uninstalling the app deletes all of it — unless you have signed in, in which case see Your account below. Individual trips can be deleted at any time from the Trips tab.

Trip backup (only if you sign in)

If you sign in with Google or Apple, each drive you record is uploaded to our database and stored against your account, so that reinstalling the app or moving to a new phone brings your drives back.

What a backed-up trip contains: the route itself — the sequence of GPS coordinates the drive followed — along with its start and end time, distance, duration, speeds, tilt figures, altitude, and any name you gave it.

A route is location data, and this is the one place the app sends your location to us. We say so plainly because the rest of this policy says we do not, and both are true depending on whether you sign in.

What it is used for: giving your drives back to you, and nothing else. It is not used for advertising or profiling, is not sold, and is not shared with data brokers. No one but you can read your trips: the database enforces, per row, that a trip is readable only by the account that recorded it.

How to avoid it entirely: continue as guest. Guests get the whole app — every gauge, the map, trip recording, trophies — with no upload of any kind.

How to delete it. Deleting a trip in the app deletes it from the database too. Deleting your account deletes every trip stored against it, along with everything else we hold for you — see How to delete your account and your data.

Signing out stops further uploads and leaves the drives on your phone. Trips already backed up stay in the database against that account until you delete them or delete the account.

Your account (only if you sign in)

Signing in is optional and the app works fully without it. If you choose Continue with Google or Continue with Apple, we hold:

  • Your email address, so the account can be recognised as yours on a new phone. If you use Apple's Hide My Email, Apple gives us a relay address instead of your real one, and that relay is all we ever see.
  • Whatever else your provider includes when it signs you in, which for Google is typically your display name and the web address of your profile picture. We do not ask for these separately; they arrive inside the sign-in token and are stored with the account.

That is the whole of it. We do not receive your password, your contacts, or anything else from your Google or Apple account, and we never post anything.

Signing in also makes your identity outlive the install — that is its purpose. Uninstalling no longer erases everything: your account and the trips backed up against it stay until you delete them.

What we collect

All of this is about the app, not about you. It is sent over an encrypted connection to our own database, and nowhere else.

An anonymous install identifier. When you first open the app it signs itself in anonymously and receives a random identifier. Until and unless you sign in it is not linked to your name, email, phone number, Google account, or advertising ID — we cannot use it to work out who you are, and neither can anyone else. It exists so that two app openings can be recognised as the same install rather than two different ones. For a guest it is created and destroyed with the install: uninstalling the app ends it permanently. If you sign in, this same identifier becomes your account and stops being disposable — see Your account above.

Basic device and app information, attached to that identifier: your phone's model, its Android version, the version of Offroad Gauges you are running, your language and region, your Offroad Gauges settings (metric or imperial, day or night theme), and the vehicle make and model you pick — which is a choice from a fixed list, not free text, and which you can skip.

How the app is used. Anonymous events recording which screens you reach and which actions you take — for example that a trip was recorded, that the Pro screen was shown, or that a trophy was earned. These carry no coordinates, no route data, and no trip names.

Whether you subscribed, as a yes/no. Payment details are handled entirely by Google Play and never reach us.

What we deliberately do not collect

  • Your location, in analytics. No coordinates, no routes and no trip names ever go into our analytics events, into advertising, or to any data broker. Routes reach our database only through the trip backup described above, only for signed-in accounts, and only to be handed back to you. A second exception is unavoidable and is not us: to draw a map, the Google Maps SDK must ask Google for the map of the area you are looking at, and on the Map tab that area is where you are. See Google Maps SDK below. Turning to a different tab stops it; nothing about the gauges, the sunset readout, or trip recording needs it.
  • Your identity, unless you sign in. As a guest we hold no account, no email address and no name for you, and we never ask for one. If you do sign in, we hold what your provider hands us — see Your account above. Your phone number and your contacts we never collect either way.
  • Your advertising ID, and no advertising or tracking networks. Offroad Gauges shows no ads and does not sell or share data with data brokers.
  • Your photos, files, microphone, or camera.

Third parties

Four Google services are involved. Their handling of the data described here is governed by Google's own privacy policy: https://policies.google.com/privacy

  • Google Maps SDK — draws the Map tab, the map behind trip cards, and the map on a shared trip image. Drawing a map means asking Google for that map, so the area being viewed is sent to Google. On the Map tab that area is centred on you, so this amounts to your approximate position; behind a trip card it is the area that trip covered. This is Google receiving it in order to render tiles, not us collecting it — we get nothing back.
  • Google Play Billing — processes Pro subscriptions and the one-time purchase. Payment details are handled entirely by Google Play; the app never sees or stores payment information.
  • Google Play — if you tap the rate prompt, it opens the app's Play Store listing.
  • Sign in with Google — only if you choose it. Google shows the account chooser, and tells us which account you picked so we can create or find your account. Google learns that you signed in to Offroad Gauges.

Apple is involved only if you choose Continue with Apple. The sign-in happens on Apple's own web page, opened in your browser; Apple tells us which account signed in, and learns that you signed in to Offroad Gauges. Apple's handling is governed by their privacy policy: https://www.apple.com/legal/privacy/

Choosing "continue as guest" involves neither of them.

Our own database is hosted by Supabase (supabase.com), which stores the install record, the anonymous usage events, your account and — if you are signed in — your backed-up trips, on our behalf and does not use any of it for its own purposes.

Sharing a trip

Sharing a trip card creates an image containing that trip's route and statistics and hands it to the app you choose. That image goes wherever you send it. Nothing is shared unless you tap Share and pick a destination, and no copy reaches us.

Permissions

  • Location (precise) — the gauges and trip recording, as described above. As a guest your position is never transmitted to us; it reaches Google only as the viewport of a map you are looking at. If you sign in, the routes of the trips you record are uploaded to us as described under Trip backup.
  • Foreground service (location) — keeps a recording running while the screen is off during a drive. The app does not request background location access, and does not record when you have not started a trip.
  • Notifications — shows the ongoing notification while a trip is recording. Declining it only hides that notification.
  • Internet — required by the Google and Supabase services listed above.

Children

Offroad Gauges is not directed at children. We do not knowingly collect personal information from anyone, including children.

Your rights

As a guest, your trips, routes and settings are yours alone and never reach us, so there is nothing on our side to export or correct — delete individual trips in the app, or uninstall to remove everything. For the anonymous install record we do hold:

  • Deleting the app deletes it. Uninstalling ends the anonymous identifier permanently. We keep no way to link a future install to a past one.
  • You can ask us to erase it sooner. Email apestudios.io@gmail.com from the device in question and we will delete the record and the identifier behind it.

Because a guest identifier is anonymous by design, we may be unable to locate a specific record from an email alone — which is a consequence of collecting as little as we do, not a refusal.

If you are signed in, we hold your account and your backed-up trips, and uninstalling no longer removes them. You can:

  • Delete individual trips in the app; each one is deleted from the database too.
  • Sign out, which stops further uploads and leaves your drives on the phone. What is already backed up stays until you delete it or delete the account.
  • Delete your account and everything in it — every trip, the account record, and the install record behind it — from inside the app or by email. See How to delete your account and your data.
  • Ask for a copy of what we hold for you, at the same address.

Data retention

Install records are kept while the install exists and for up to 24 months after it stops being seen, then deleted. Usage events are attached to the install record and are deleted with it.

Changes

Material changes to this policy will be posted here with an updated effective date, and, where required, announced in the app's Play Store listing.
