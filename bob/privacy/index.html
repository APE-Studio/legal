Privacy Policy

Bob: Cynic Travel Guide — APE Studios

Effective date: 1 September 2026 Developer: APE Studios Contact: apestudios.io@gmail.com Published at: https://ape-studio.github.io/legal/bob/privacy/

Bob is a travel guide: a map, a button, and a narrator who tells you about the places around you with a dry sense of humour. This policy describes what the app does with your data.

The short version: your location is used to show you a map and to find places near you, and it is never stored as any kind of history. Signing in is optional — as a guest we hold nothing about you at all. If you sign in with Apple, we get your email address and, once, your name — and nothing else. There are no ads, no analytics, no trackers, and no data brokers anywhere in this app.

How to delete your account and your data

This applies if you signed in — a guest has no account to delete, and nothing of a guest exists on our side in the first place. There are two ways, and either one erases everything we hold for you — your account and the profile stored with it.

In the app. The gear button → Account → Delete my account. The app asks you to confirm with Apple one more time (that is Apple's own re-authentication, and it lets us disconnect the app from your Apple Account at the same moment), and then it is done immediately. Deleting your account does not cancel a subscription — the app offers you Apple's subscription management screen for that, and you can always reach it in the iPhone's Settings under your name → Subscriptions.

By email, if you no longer have the app. Write to apestudios.io@gmail.com from the address you signed in with and ask us to delete your account. You do not need a reason, and you do not need to reinstall anything. We will confirm when it is done. If you signed in with Hide My Email, write from the relay address Apple created — that is the address we know you by.

Your account (only if you sign in)

Signing in is optional — the app offers "continue as guest", and a guest gets the app without any account existing anywhere: we hold no identifier, no email, and no name for you, and we never ask for one.

If you choose to sign in with Apple, Apple shows its own sheet, you approve with Face ID, and this is what we end up holding:

  • An identifier for your account — a random ID, created at sign-up.
  • Your email address, so the account can be recognised as yours on a new phone. Apple's Hide My Email works fully: if you choose it, we only ever see the relay address, and that is fine with us.
  • Your name, which Apple hands over exactly once, at the first sign-in, if you agree to share it. We use it to say hello and for nothing else.

We never see your password — Apple does not give it to anyone, including us. We do not receive your contacts, your photos, or anything else from your Apple Account.

Your location

Location is what makes Bob work, so here is exactly where it goes. The app uses your position only while you are using it — it never requests background location, and it never records where you have been.

  • To Apple, to draw the map. The map is Apple Maps, and drawing a map means asking Apple for the area you are looking at — which, when the map is centred on you, amounts to your approximate position. Apple also tells the app which country the map is in, so the narrator can speak like a local. This is governed by Apple's privacy policy, not ours; we get nothing from it.
  • To Wikipedia (the Wikimedia Foundation), to find places. When you tap the button, the app sends your coordinates to Wikipedia's public search API and gets back the nearby places it knows about. Wikipedia receives the coordinates the way any website receives a request; it does not know who you are.
  • To our own database, to check for hand-prepared guides. The app asks our database whether we have pre-written narrations for the area shown on the map, and that question necessarily contains the area's coordinates. The request is answered and that is the end of it: it is not linked to your account, and we do not build any record of the places you look at or visit.

If you decline location access, the map cannot centre on you and nearby search will not work — the app is honest about needing it.

Narration

When Bob talks about a place, the writing and the voice may be generated in the cloud:

  • The app sends the place's name (and the country code) to our server, which fetches the place's Wikipedia article and has an AI model (via OpenAI) write the narration from it, and — in cloud voice mode — turn that text into speech.
  • What is sent is the place, never you: these requests carry no account identity, no email, and no device identifier. OpenAI processes the text to produce the result and is not permitted to use it for advertising.
  • The on-device voice mode (the default) speaks with the iPhone's own synthesizer — in that mode, nothing about the narration leaves your phone except the Wikipedia lookups above.

Subscriptions and payment

Bob's subscription is bought through Apple's App Store. Apple processes the payment entirely: we never see or store your payment details, and we do not even keep a record of your subscription on our servers — your iPhone proves the subscription to the app directly. The free-listen counter lives on your device. Cancelling works the way every App Store subscription does, in Settings under your name → Subscriptions.

What we deliberately do not collect

  • No location history. Nothing you look at, tap, or listen to is stored against you or anyone.
  • No analytics and no trackers. The app contains no analytics SDK, no advertising ID collection, no ads, and no crash-reporting service.
  • No microphone, camera, contacts, or photos. Bob talks; it does not listen.

Third parties

  • Apple — Sign in with Apple, the map, and App Store payments, each described above. Governed by Apple's privacy policy.
  • Wikimedia Foundation — the nearby-place search and the article summaries and photos the app shows. Governed by the Wikimedia privacy policy.
  • OpenAI — generates narration text and the cloud voice, as a processor behind our own server; it receives place names and article text, never your identity.
  • Supabase (supabase.com) — hosts our database and server functions: your account, the prepared place guides, and the narration proxy. It stores this on our behalf and does not use any of it for its own purposes.

Permissions

  • Location (while using the app) — the map and nearby search, as described above. Never in the background.
  • Background audio — narration keeps playing with the screen locked. This is playback only; it involves no data and no microphone.
  • Internet — required by the services listed above.

Children

Bob is not directed at children. We do not knowingly collect personal information from anyone, including children.

Your rights

As a guest there is nothing on our side to export, correct, or delete — that is the point of the guest option. If you signed in, you can:

  • Delete your account and everything in it, from inside the app or by email — see How to delete your account and your data.
  • Ask for a copy of what we hold for you — which is your account row: the identifier, the email, and the name if you shared it — at apestudios.io@gmail.com.
  • Correct it at the same address.

Data retention

Your account is kept until you delete it, and deleting it removes the profile stored with it at the same moment. Ordinary technical server logs (the kind every web service produces) are short-lived and are not used to build any profile of you.
